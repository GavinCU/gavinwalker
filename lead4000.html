PRLC 1810 — Leadership Foundations and Applications I

This introductory course established a foundation for understanding what leadership actually is. Moving beyond the idea that leadership simply means being in charge. The course explored a wide range of leadership styles and frameworks, examining how different approaches apply across different environments, communities, and challenges in the real world.

Knowledge & Skills Gained

The most significant takeaway was learning that no single leadership style fits every situation. Understanding how to read a context and adapt accordingly.

PRLC 1820 — Leadership Foundations and Applications II

Building directly on the first foundations course, PRLC 1820 shifted the focus from individual leadership styles to the dynamics of leading within groups and teams. The course explored how leaders cultivate collaboration, navigate group tension, and create environments where people can do their best work together.

Knowledge & Skills Gained

This course gave me practical language and frameworks for something I was already doing intuitively. PRLC 1820 helped me understand the theory behind what makes teams function and gave me tools to be more intentional about how I show up in collective spaces.

ENLP 3100 — Complex Leadership Challenges

ENLP 3100 marked a shift into deeper, more challenging territory. Through intensive readings and reflective work, the course asked students to examine what it truly means to lead well not just in practice but in character. The focus was on self-reflection as an ongoing leadership practice, pushing past surface-level answers to sit with harder questions about identity, values, and growth.

Knowledge & Skills Gained

The readings in this course were the most challenging I had encountered in the minor and the most rewarding. They pushed me to interrogate my own assumptions about what a good leader looks like and recognize the ways I had internalized narratives that did not fully reflect who I want to be.

PRLC 3800 — Global Inquiry and Leadership

PRLC 3800 introduced the concept of "wicked problems" complex, systemic issues that resist straightforward solutions and require leaders to think across disciplines, timelines, and perspectives. The course examined what it means to lead when there is no clear answer, only a set of tradeoffs, stakeholders, and competing values to navigate responsibly.

Knowledge & Skills Gained

Understanding wicked problems reframed how I think about leadership entirely. It taught me that the ability to hold complexity without needing to resolve it quickly is itself a leadership skill. That thinking carries directly into my LEAD 4000 practicum, where my team is analyzing fraud in Boulder County.

LEAD 4000 — Leadership in Context & Emerging Challenges: A Capstone

LEAD 4000 is the capstone course for the Leadership Studies Minor at CU Boulder. A praxis-based seminar that integrates leadership theory, personal reflection, and real-world action. The course challenges students to work within complex systems, engage in root cause analysis, and apply change strategies in partnership with a real community organization.

Current Focus — Spring 2026

My team is currently past the halfway point of the semester, focused on fraud in Boulder County as our systems issue. We are conducting stakeholder interviews, root cause analysis, and issue mapping to understand the structures that enable and perpetuate financial fraud in the community. This work is pushing me to synthesize everything across the minor.
